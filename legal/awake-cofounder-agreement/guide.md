# Drafting and completion guide

This guide is educational and non-operative. It is not part of the agreement and must not be attached or incorporated by reference unless counsel intentionally directs otherwise. Read the [disclaimer](./DISCLAIMER.md) first.

## What this template is—and is not

The template assumes one combined structure:

1. a California LLC acts as the founders' governance and holding vehicle; and
2. that LLC owns securities of a Delaware corporation used for operations and fundraising.

It is not a standalone California LLC operating agreement, a standalone Delaware founders' agreement, or a menu from which users should casually mix clauses. The structure can affect tax treatment, qualified small business stock eligibility, securities compliance, fiduciary duties, investor acceptance, and ownership records. Confirm the structure with California and Delaware startup counsel and tax advisers before forming entities or issuing equity.

## Field legend

- `[REQUIRED: …]` — must be completed before signature.
- `[SELECT ONE: …]` — choose one option and delete the others.
- `[OPTIONAL: …]` — keep only after an informed decision.
- `[COUNSEL: …]` — requires individualized legal or tax review.
- `[DRAFTING NOTE: …]` — instruction only; remove before signature.

The canonical agreement retains visible bracketed fields because it is a template. A signed copy must not contain unresolved brackets, examples, field labels, or drafting notes.

## Completion checklist

- Confirm legal names, addresses, entity names, entity types, and jurisdictions.
- Confirm the California LLC and Delaware corporation have actually been formed and are in good standing.
- Reconcile capitalization, founder percentages, contributions, securities records, and the cap table.
- Decide whether founder interests vest; specify commencement dates, cliffs, schedules, acceleration, repurchase rights, and consideration.
- Inventory background intellectual property and execute any separate invention-assignment and confidentiality agreements counsel recommends.
- Define roles, time commitments, authority, bank access, statutory-filing responsibility, and information delivery.
- Set approval thresholds, reserved matters, debt and litigation limits, and deadlock mechanics.
- Complete transfer, departure, valuation, repurchase, and payment terms.
- Review any restraint, noncompetition, nonsolicitation, confidentiality, and trade-secret language for current enforceability.
- Select mediation, arbitration, seat, rules, notices, and governing-law details.
- Obtain board, member, stockholder, and other approvals required by the entities' governing documents and applicable law.
- Obtain securities and tax advice, including advice on any time-sensitive elections or filings.
- Remove all instructions and unresolved fields; verify every schedule and signature block.

## Concepts founders should understand

### Vesting and ownership

Issued ownership describes what the entity has legally granted. Vested ownership describes the portion no longer subject to a contractual repurchase or forfeiture mechanism. Fully diluted ownership generally models outstanding equity plus specified rights to acquire equity. These concepts are not interchangeable, and the agreement must match the actual issuance documents and capitalization records.

### Departures

A founder's operational departure does not automatically determine what happens to issued equity. The agreement, equity-purchase documents, vesting terms, repurchase rights, employment arrangements, and governing law must work together.

### Intellectual property

Background IP is material owned before or outside the venture. Venture-created IP is material created for the venture. A general assignment clause may not replace separate invention-assignment documents, confirmatory assignments, source-code and data records, or consents required to transfer third-party material.

### Governance and deadlock

Reserved matters define decisions that cannot be made through ordinary authority. Deadlock provisions determine what happens when required approval is unavailable. Board duties in a Delaware corporation may not be displaced merely by an agreement among LLC members.

### Tax posture

LLCs and corporations are taxed differently, and a holding structure can affect founders and the operating company. Equity issued for services or subject to vesting can create tax and filing consequences. Get advice before issuance, contribution, transfer, or election.

## Formation and transaction package

Depending on the facts, the agreement may need to be coordinated with:

- articles of organization and a California LLC operating agreement;
- a Delaware certificate of incorporation and bylaws;
- incorporator, board, stockholder, manager, or member consents;
- founder stock-purchase or membership-interest purchase agreements;
- capitalization records, stock ledgers, membership ledgers, and certificates;
- invention-assignment, confidentiality, employment, and contractor agreements;
- securities notices, exemptions, and filings;
- vesting, repurchase, transfer, and equity-plan documents;
- intercompany IP, services, licensing, or cost-sharing agreements; and
- federal, state, and local tax registrations, elections, and filings.

Signing this agreement does not by itself complete any of those actions.
