Limited Liability Company Operating Agreement of
[REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] COFOUNDERS LLC
a California limited liability company (Cal. Corp. Code § 17701.01 et seq.), governing the Cofounders' interests in the Venture and in [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] Platform Corporation, a Delaware corporation

Dated: [REQUIRED: EFFECTIVE DATE]

1. Parties

This Awake Cofounder Agreement (the “Agreement”) is entered into on [REQUIRED: EFFECTIVE DATE] by and between:

(a) [REQUIRED: NAME OF COFOUNDER 1], residing at [REQUIRED: NOTICE ADDRESS] (the “Majority Cofounder”); and

(b) [REQUIRED: NAME OF COFOUNDER 2], residing at [REQUIRED: NOTICE ADDRESS] (the “Minority Cofounder”), each a “Member” or “Cofounder” and together the “Members” or “Cofounders”. The persons identified in paragraphs (a) and (b) are the “Original Cofounders.” An additional LLC Member may be admitted only under Clause 11.1 and must be recorded in Schedule A and execute a deed of adherence. A Later Participant under Clause 11.3 is not a Member solely by receiving Company equity or a cofounder title.

2. Recitals

2.1 The Cofounders wish to come together to build and scale a new venture under the name [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] (the “Venture”), operated through the Company and any approved operating Group Entities, with the Original Cofounders’ shared equity held through [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] Cofounders LLC, a limited liability company organized or to be organized under the California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act, Cal. Corp. Code § 17701.01 et seq. (“RULLCA”) (the “LLC”). The Company is [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] Platform Corporation, incorporated or to be incorporated in Delaware.

2.2 The Cofounders intend that this Agreement governs their equity, economic entitlements, governance rights, and obligations toward one another — both within the LLC and, through the LLC’s holding and exercise of securities, within every Group Entity — for the duration of the Venture, and that it may be changed only by mutual agreement as set out in Clause 17.

2.3 The Cofounders specifically acknowledge that the Minority Cofounder is joining to help scale the Venture to a higher valuation, and that the protections in this Agreement — in particular Clauses 6, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 17 — exist so that no Member, regardless of the size of their Membership Interest, can unilaterally alter the equity, economic rights, or governance rights of any other Member, whether at the level of the LLC or of any Group Entity.

3. Definitions

“Capital Contribution” means the cash, property, intellectual property, or services contributed by a Member to the LLC, as recorded in Schedule A.

“Company” means [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] Platform Corporation, a Delaware corporation, and any successor to it.

“Fair Market Value” means the value of a Membership Interest determined by an independent valuer jointly appointed by the Members (or, failing agreement within 30 days, appointed under the dispute-resolution process in Clause 19), valuing the LLC — including the look-through value of all Group Entity Securities it holds — as a going concern without minority or marketability discounts.

“Founder Equity” means the Membership Interests held by the original Cofounders identified in Clause 1, and excludes interests issued to investors, advisors, employees, option holders, or other subsequent participants. References to a percentage of Founder Equity are measured against the total Membership Interests held by the original Cofounders, so that dilution caused by the admission of investors or other later participants does not, by itself, reduce a Cofounder’s percentage of Founder Equity.

“Group Entity” means the Company and any other entity in which the LLC holds securities directly or indirectly in connection with the Venture. For the anti-circumvention obligations in Clauses 6.6 and 9.5, a successor or other entity used by a Member to carry on, receive, or divert the Venture’s business, assets, or opportunities is also covered, whether or not the LLC then holds securities in it; this does not itself confer ownership or bind that entity.

“Group Entity Securities” means all shares, options, warrants, convertible instruments, and other securities of any Group Entity held by the LLC from time to time.

“Membership Interest” means a Member’s entire ownership interest in the LLC, including rights to profits, losses, distributions, and governance rights, expressed as a percentage in Schedule A, and including that Member’s indirect pro-rata interest in all Group Entity Securities.

“Original Founder Block” means the Group Entity Securities held by the LLC for the Members under this Agreement, including securities acquired in substitution, conversion, or exchange and their proceeds; it does not include properly approved separately held securities of Later Participants.

“Later Participant” means a person other than an Original Cofounder, and not acting as a nominee or controlled vehicle of one, admitted as a later founder, employee, adviser, or investor at Company level under Clause 11.3. A vehicle’s tax classification must be documented; its ownership does not itself establish QSBS eligibility.

“QSBS” means stock potentially eligible for treatment under Section 1202 of the Internal Revenue Code. Use of this term describes a planning objective, not a representation that any security or Member qualifies.

“Tax Coordinator” means the Member designated in Schedule C to coordinate records and professional advice under Clause 21, without authority to change protected rights or make a Member’s personal tax elections.

“Reserved Matters” means the matters listed in Schedule B (Part 1 for the LLC and Part 2 for Group Entities), which require the consent described in Clause 8.

“Supermajority Consent” means the prior written consent of Members holding at least [REQUIRED: SUPERMAJORITY THRESHOLD]% of the issued Membership Interests, which must include the written consent of the Minority Cofounder for any matter identified in Schedule B as requiring Minority Consent.

“Unanimous Consent” means the prior written consent of every Member.

4. The LLC and the Venture

4.1 Name and Operating Assets. The Venture shall use [REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] or another name approved under Schedule B. Operating trade names, brands, goodwill, contracts, and Venture IP shall be owned by the Company or an approved operating Group Entity, subject to Clause 14. No Member owns those assets individually merely by contributing to the Venture.

4.2 Formation, Filings, and Principal Office. The Members shall cause Articles of Organization for the LLC to be filed, and thereafter maintained, with the California Secretary of State under RULLCA, shall designate and maintain an agent for service of process in California, shall file the initial and biennial Statements of Information when due, and shall pay when due the annual franchise tax and any limited liability company fee imposed under the California Revenue and Taxation Code. The principal office of the LLC shall be at [REQUIRED: CALIFORNIA PRINCIPAL OFFICE].

4.3 Holding Purpose. The LLC shall hold, manage, vote, and exercise the Original Founder Block, administer this Agreement, and perform incidental holding-vehicle activities, including professional engagements and tax compliance. It shall not conduct the Venture’s customer-facing operations, employ its operating workforce, hold operating IP as a separate business, or assume operating guarantees without Unanimous Consent after legal and tax review. This restriction does not excuse mandatory filings or make the LLC exempt from taxes or other applicable law.

4.4 Duration. The LLC continues for the duration of the Venture until dissolved in accordance with this Agreement and applicable law. This Agreement remains in force for so long as a Member holds a Membership Interest, unless changed by mutual agreement under Clause 17.

4.5 Limited Liability. In accordance with Cal. Corp. Code § 17703.04, the debts, obligations, and other liabilities of the LLC, whether arising in contract, tort, or otherwise, are solely those of the LLC, and no Member is personally liable for them, or for the acts or omissions of any other Member, solely by reason of being or acting as a Member. Each Member remains responsible for their own wrongful acts and for any obligation they personally guarantee.

5. Capital Contributions and Equity

5.1 Each Member shall provide the contribution and consideration recorded in Schedule A. The schedule shall identify the recipient entity, the property or services, legal ownership and tax treatment, and actual transfer date. Any IP contribution is subject to Clause 14. An agreed commercial value is not automatically fair market value or tax basis, and services alone do not establish stock issuance, tax ownership, or a QSBS holding period. Separate authorized LLC and Company issuance documents are required.

5.2 The Membership Interests of the Members are as set out in Schedule A. Membership Interests determine each Member’s share of profits, losses, and distributions — including all economic value flowing from Group Entity Securities — unless this Agreement expressly provides otherwise.

5.3 No Mandatory Further Contributions. No Member is obliged to make any further Capital Contribution without their own written consent. A Member’s refusal to make a further contribution shall not, by itself, reduce that Member’s Membership Interest except through a new issuance carried out in full compliance with Clauses 6 and 8.

5.4 Coordinated Implementation. Before the relevant services, contributions, or equity transfers begin, the Members shall approve the Schedule D closing checklist and obtain execution by each responsible entity or rights holder of the necessary implementing documents. Identify the formation, approvals, consideration, actual member tax ownership, issuance, assignment or license, ledger, and election steps, their responsible persons, and target dates. If material services or asset transfers begin before completion, a written interim arrangement signed by the affected persons and responsible entities must specify the interim scope, compensation or lawful alternative, IP treatment, issuance completion date, and remedies or permitted suspension if completion is delayed. It must not require unlawful unpaid work, backdate a transfer, imply an unapproved issuance, or authorize withholding company credentials or existing lawful access. Noncompletion preserves accrued contractual and statutory remedies; it does not create or forfeit equity by implication. Counsel shall reconcile the interim arrangement with Clause 14 before signing so title and transfer timing are explicit.

6. Equity Protection and Anti-Dilution

6.1 No Unilateral Change to Equity. No Member’s Membership Interest, profit share, distribution rights, or governance rights may be reduced, cancelled, reallocated, subordinated, or otherwise adversely altered without that Member’s prior written consent — whether by action taken within the LLC or by action taken in or through any Group Entity. Any purported change made without such consent is void and of no effect as between the Members.

6.2 New Issuances. The creation or issuance of any new Membership Interest (including to a new partner, investor, employee, or advisor) is a Reserved Matter. Any permitted issuance shall dilute all Members pro rata to their then-current Membership Interests, unless every affected Member consents in writing to a different treatment.

6.3 Pre-emptive Rights. Before any new Membership Interest is issued, each existing Member has the right (but not the obligation) to subscribe for their pro-rata share of the new interest on the same terms, exercisable within [REQUIRED: 30 OR OTHER] days of written notice setting out the price and terms.

6.4 Equal Treatment. Any issuance, repurchase, redemption, distribution, or restructuring must treat all Members of the same class identically on a per-percentage basis. The LLC shall not repurchase or redeem any interest from one Member without offering the same terms pro rata to all Members, except vesting repurchases expressly agreed under Clause 6.5, buyouts expressly permitted under Clauses 12, 13, or 16, and mandatory withholding or reconciliations under Clauses 7.4–7.5.

6.5 Vesting (complete the election with counsel). If the Members elect vesting, each Member’s Membership Interest vests as set out in Schedule A. Unvested interests of a departing Member may be repurchased at the lower of cost and Fair Market Value; vested interests may only ever be repurchased at Fair Market Value and only in the circumstances expressly permitted by this Agreement. Schedule A must affirmatively select no vesting or state the complete vesting and repurchase terms; no vesting or acceleration schedule takes effect by default. Before issuance, counsel and the tax preparer shall document in Schedule D the relevant property transfer, partner-status and ownership analysis, consideration, and any Section 83(b) or other election at each relevant level. No retroactive document date or assumed election may substitute for actual compliance. Before implementing forfeiture or repurchase, obtain the review required by Clause 21.4.

6.6 Separate Economics and Circumvention. Neither Original Cofounder may acquire or arrange venture shares, options, convertible rights, carried interests, revenue participation, or other venture economics outside the Original Founder Block, personally or through a nominee, family member, or controlled vehicle, without Unanimous Consent specifying the terms and their effect on the other Members. The same approval is required for a diversion to a successor or affiliated business. This does not prohibit compensation expressly approved under Clause 7.3, reimbursement of documented authorized expenses, or a transfer complying with Clause 12.5. Later Participant permissions do not create an exception for an Original Cofounder.

6.7 Optional Milestone Awards. Base founder interests and any additional milestone award shall be separately identified in Schedule A. A milestone award requires a fully completed award record, the approvals required by this Agreement, and implementing issuer documents. An incomplete proposal creates no milestone award or additional forfeiture condition on an existing interest; it does not cancel a previously valid award. A milestone percentage must identify its denominator, actual instrument, issuer, recipient, maximum quantity, achievement conditions, and any subsequent service vesting. No new roadmap, estimate, or unilateral acceptance instruction may enlarge an approved equity condition. Changes require the affected Member’s written consent and all other applicable approvals. Apply Clause 21 before an award, ownership increase, forfeiture, or repurchase; economic achievement does not itself establish tax ownership of an earlier stock lot.

6.8 Milestone Acceptance and Disputes. Each completed award record shall state objective criteria, evidence, submission and response deadlines, funding and staffing commitments, external dependencies, and the selected treatment of delay, partial achievement, termination, and acquisition. The appointed reviewer shall act reasonably and in good faith and give specific written reasons for rejection. Silence neither certifies achievement nor authorizes forfeiture. A timely disputed submission shall be referred promptly to a jointly selected independent expert with relevant expertise; if selection is not agreed within 10 business days of written referral, use the appointment process under Clause 19. The expert determines factual compliance with the agreed criteria, dependency events, and achievement date, subject to review for fraud, material conflict, or manifest error and mandatory law. The expert cannot rewrite terms, create an award, supply missing consent, or change protected economics. Legal and contractual interpretation disputes remain under Clause 19. Preserve the disputed portion pending determination; administer undisputed portions. Correct vesting credit to the factual achievement date under the agreed terms without backdating an actual issuance, and apply subsequent service conditions as expressly selected. The relevant issuer must adopt this process in its award documents.

7. Profits, Losses, and Distributions

7.1 Economic and Tax Allocations. Economic profits and losses shall follow the Membership Interests, subject to the expressly agreed terms. Tax allocations and capital accounts shall be maintained under applicable partnership tax law, including Sections 704(b), 704(c), and 706 where relevant. Required tax allocations, contributed-property differences, or differing QSBS eligibility do not themselves change contractual ownership, voting rights, or distribution entitlements. Before any noncash contribution, non-pro-rata economic arrangement, or ownership change, the tax preparer shall identify necessary allocation and capital-account provisions for approval under Clause 17; no preparer may amend the bargain by bookkeeping entry.

7.2 Distributions. Subject to lawful-distribution limits, documented reasonable reserves, Clause 7.4, and mandatory withholding under Clause 7.5, distributions shall be made at the times and in the amounts approved under Clause 8 and pro rata to Membership Interests. No discretionary distribution may favor one Member without the consents required by Clauses 6 and 17. Corporate distributions to the LLC remain subject to Company approvals and law; this Agreement does not itself compel the Company to declare a dividend.

7.3 Remuneration. Any salary, drawing, management fee, bonus, or other compensation paid to a Member by the LLC or by any Group Entity (beyond pro-rata distributions and arm’s-length employment compensation approved under Schedule B) must be approved as a Reserved Matter and recorded in writing. This prevents a majority Member from extracting value through compensation in place of distributions.

7.4 Pro Rata Tax Advances. The LLC shall make quarterly cash tax advances, to the extent lawfully available after documented reasonable reserves, under the following common methodology. The independent tax preparer shall calculate cumulative assumed federal and California tax attributable to the LLC’s year-to-date income and gains using a common set of the highest marginal individual rates applicable to each income category, including net investment income tax where applicable, and taking account of state nonconformity. A federal QSBS exclusion may reduce the common calculation only to the extent the preparer reasonably supports its availability to every Member for the relevant gain. Personal deductions, credits, residency differences, and unrelated losses shall not produce individualized payments. This is a common cash-planning convention and not any Member’s return calculation.

(a) The aggregate additional advance equals the positive excess of that cumulative assumed tax over prior cash distributions and tax advances for that year, including amounts treated as distributions under Clause 7.5. Pay that aggregate amount pro rata to the Membership Interests applicable to the distribution. The preparer shall deliver the calculation at least 15 days before the applicable individual estimated-tax due date; payment shall be made at least five business days before that date when lawfully available. Reasonable estimates may be used and later reconciled. The Tax Coordinator shall promptly disclose any expected shortfall and the cash and reserve calculation; unpaid targets are reviewed each quarter and are not guaranteed funding obligations.

(b) The preparer shall reconcile estimates within 90 days after year end and when final tax information materially changes them. A positive shortfall is paid on the same basis when lawfully available. Excess advances reduce later amounts otherwise distributable to the same Member and are not additional economics; absent an unlawful distribution or a separately agreed obligation, they create no personal repayment obligation. Record all advances and offsets so they are counted only once. Before an ownership change or final liquidation, approve a settlement statement under the otherwise applicable consent requirements to preserve the agreed economics and prevent double crediting.

(c) The Members authorize payments calculated under this clause without a new discretionary distribution vote. Establishing or materially increasing reserves, changing the common methodology, or making a non-pro-rata adjustment requires Unanimous Consent; mandatory legal limits still apply without a vote. No Member is guaranteed cash equal to their actual tax, and no individual tax gross-up or cross-subsidy is created. The Tax Coordinator shall retain the preparer’s common rate, income-category, exclusion, reserve, and reconciliation records in Schedule D’s supporting file.

7.5 Mandatory Withholding. Amounts that applicable law requires the LLC to withhold or pay specifically on behalf of a Member shall be treated as distributed to that Member and credited against that Member’s entitlement. Corresponding cash payments to other Members shall preserve pro rata total economic distributions to the extent legally and financially possible; any shortfall is recorded and reconciled before further discretionary distributions. This administrative treatment is not authority to adopt an elective entity-level tax or alter economic rights without the required approvals.

8. Governance and Decision-Making

8.1 Day-to-Day Matters. The LLC is member-managed. Decisions in the ordinary course of business may be taken by the Member(s) designated in Schedule C or by Members holding a simple majority of the issued Membership Interests, acting in good faith and consistently with the approved budget and business plan.

8.2 Reserved Matters. Notwithstanding Clause 8.1, none of the Reserved Matters listed in Schedule B may be undertaken — whether by the LLC directly or through the exercise (or deliberate non-exercise) of rights attaching to Group Entity Securities —without the consent level specified in Schedule B (Unanimous Consent, or Supermajority Consent including Minority Consent). This Clause 8.2 is the principal protection ensuring no Member can unilaterally affect the equity or rights of another Member.

8.3 Information Rights. Every Member is entitled to: (a) monthly management accounts of the LLC and each Group Entity within [REQUIRED: 15 OR OTHER] days of month end; (b) annual financial statements; (c) reasonable access during business hours to the books, records, contracts, bank statements, cap-table records, and stock ledgers of the LLC and each Group Entity (to the extent within the LLC’s control); and (d) prompt notice of any material event, litigation, or proposed Reserved Matter. These rights may not be reduced without the consent of the Member concerned.

8.4 Meetings. Any Member may convene a Members’ meeting on [REQUIRED: 7 OR OTHER] days’ written notice. Quorum requires every Member (or their proxy); if quorum fails twice, the adjourned meeting may proceed with the Members present, but no Reserved Matter may be decided without the consents required by Schedule B.

8.5 Filing Responsibility. The Members named in Schedule C shall serve as the Members responsible for statutory filings and compliance of the LLC. Appointment and removal of such a Member is a Reserved Matter. Serving in that capacity confers no additional equity or voting power, and does not make the LLC manager-managed.

8.6 Good Faith and Fiduciary Duty. Each Member owes the LLC and the other Members duties of good faith, loyalty, and full disclosure — which supplement, and do not replace, the fiduciary duties and the contractual obligation of good faith and fair dealing under Cal. Corp. Code § 17704.09, which under RULLCA may be shaped but not eliminated — and shall not use majority position, control of bank accounts, control of registrations, or any office held in a Group Entity to disadvantage any other Member.

9. Group Entities and the Company

9.1 Structure. The Venture is conducted through a two-tier structure: this LLC is the Cofounders’ governance and holding vehicle, and the Company ([REQUIRED: VENTURE NAME] Platform Corporation, a Delaware corporation) is the principal operating and fundraising vehicle. The internal corporate affairs of the Company are governed by Delaware law; this Agreement governs how the LLC and the Members exercise their own rights in respect of the Company and every other Group Entity.

9.2 Original Founder Block. All venture securities acquired for or by the Original Cofounders shall be issued to and held of record by the LLC, unless Unanimous Consent expressly approves an exception under Clause 6.6 or Clause 12.8. Approved Later Participants may hold separately issued securities directly or through their disclosed vehicles under Clause 11.3. Such holdings do not confer LLC membership or permit an Original Cofounder to hold outside the block. Any exception for an Original Cofounder requires equivalent protective corporate agreements to take effect before the transfer or issuance.

9.3 Exercise of Securities Is a Reserved Matter. Any vote, written consent, waiver, election, conversion, exercise, or transfer of, or other action or deliberate omission in respect of, Group Entity Securities concerning any matter listed in Schedule B Part 2 requires the consent specified there. No Member, officer, or agent of the LLC may sign, vote, or deliver any such instrument on behalf of the LLC without that consent, and any instrument signed in breach is unauthorized.

9.4 Minority Cofounder Board Seat. The Minority Cofounder shall be entitled to designate one director to the board of the Company for so long as the Minority Cofounder holds at least [REQUIRED: BOARD-RIGHT THRESHOLD]% of the outstanding Founder Equity. The LLC shall vote and exercise its Group Entity Securities, and the Members shall take all actions reasonably within their power, to cause that nominee’s election and to oppose that nominee’s removal other than for cause. This nomination right may not be reduced without the Minority Cofounder’s written consent. Because it is measured against Founder Equity rather than fully-diluted equity, ordinary dilution from admitting investors or other later participants does not, by itself, terminate this right.

9.5 No Circumvention. No Member shall use any position as a director, officer, employee, or advisor of any Group Entity to accomplish indirectly anything this Agreement prohibits the Members from doing directly (including diluting, diminishing, or restructuring another Member’s direct or indirect interest). If a conflict arises between this Agreement and the organizational documents of any Group Entity, then, as between the Members and to the maximum extent lawful, this Agreement prevails, and the Members shall exercise all rights within their power (including voting the Group Entity Securities) to amend the conflicting documents or otherwise give effect to this Agreement. The Members acknowledge that directors of the Company owe fiduciary duties under Delaware law and nothing in this Agreement requires any director to breach those duties; where such duties prevent an action, the Members shall implement the intent of this Agreement by lawful alternative means.

9.6 Look-Through Economics and Exits. Amounts received by the LLC in respect of Group Entity Securities shall be applied to lawful obligations and approved reserves and distributed under Clause 7, including reconciliation of prior tax advances. Transfers of Group Entity Securities are subject to Clause 12 as applicable to the asset and transaction, with Member-level economic participation reflected in the LLC proceeds. A sale by the LLC of corporate stock and a sale by Members of LLC interests are distinct transactions for tax purposes; use Clause 12.7 before choosing the form.

9.7 Implementation at Company Level. Before initial Company stock issuance, the Members shall obtain the legal and tax review required by Clause 21 and cause appropriate charter, bylaw, stockholders’, voting, and equity-issuance documents to implement the protected consent rights, board nomination, information access, transfer safeguards, and anti-circumvention obligations of this Agreement to the extent lawful. Record these documents in Schedule D. Before later issuances or financing, require appropriate Company and new-holder execution or joinders and confirm continued protection. Persons who have not agreed are not bound merely by this Agreement. Corporate powers, mandatory law, and directors’ duties must be respected; an internal LLC restriction is not a substitute for necessary corporate provisions.

10. Roles, Responsibilities, and Commitment

10.1 Each Member’s role, title, and expected time commitment — in the LLC and in any Group Entity — are set out in Schedule C. Changes to a Member’s own role require that Member’s consent.

10.2 No Removal Without Cause. A Member may be removed from their operational role (in the LLC or in any Group Entity, to the extent within the Members’ control) only for Cause, meaning: fraud; material and persistent breach of this Agreement unremedied [REQUIRED: 30 OR OTHER] days after written notice; conviction of a serious criminal offence; or sustained incapacity exceeding [REQUIRED: 6 OR OTHER] months. Removal for Cause requires the affirmative vote of all other Members and does not, by itself, divest the removed Member of any vested Membership Interest, which remains subject to Clause 13.

10.3 Service Terms and Authority. Schedule C and coordinated entity service agreements shall expressly state duties, time commitment, availability, reporting, delegated spending and hiring authority, compensation and commencement, benefits and expenses, resources, and permitted outside activities. A blank does not mean unpaid service, unlimited availability, or authority to bind an entity. Any selection of no cash compensation or deferred compensation is subject to mandatory employment, wage, tax, and other law and must identify the lawful arrangement and responsible entity. No schedule waives nonwaivable rights. Material changes to a Member’s role or protected rights require the consents already specified in this Agreement.

10.4 Operational Departure and Optional Benefits. Service cessation does not itself cancel vested ownership or information rights or create a new buyout right. Clauses 6, 12, 13, and 16 continue to govern their express vesting and exit events. Schedule C must expressly select no additional contractual severance or complete severance terms; Schedule A must expressly select no acceleration or complete acceleration terms, including the treatment of termination, any defined Good Reason, death, disability, and acquisition. A remedy for a prohibited removal does not authorize that removal. No incomplete selection creates an optional benefit or forfeiture; statutory rights and remedies for breach remain available.

10.5 Companion Documents. Members shall cause the responsible entities to execute service and equity documents consistent with the protected founder bargain, within their lawful authority and subject to directors’ duties. A service document, later financing form, or entire-agreement clause shall not amend this Agreement between the Members without Clause 17 consent. Identify the implementing instruments and any expressly approved amendment in Schedule D. Do not represent an unsigned entity as bound or displace mandatory corporate powers.

11. Admission of New Members and Investors

11.1 LLC Admissions. Admission of any new Member, investor, or holder of an economic or governance interest in the LLC requires Unanimous Consent, updated Schedules A and D, a deed of adherence, and advance tax review of existing stock lots and ownership changes. Company-level participation is governed separately by Clause 11.3 and does not automatically admit anyone to the LLC.

11.2 Any new Member must execute a deed of adherence agreeing to be bound by this Agreement before acquiring any interest.

11.3 Later Participants. The default is for a Later Participant to receive separately issued Company securities directly or through a disclosed personal holding vehicle, subject to the issuance approvals in Schedule B, securities compliance, and appropriate corporate agreements and joinders under Clause 9.7. Each proposal shall identify the recipient and beneficial owner, consideration, vesting, tax classification of any vehicle, effect on the Original Founder Block, and whether QSBS is intended for that issuance. Buying outstanding shares is not equivalent to original issuance. Admission into the LLC instead requires a separate unanimous decision under Clause 11.1. No issuance approval waives an affected Member’s protected rights.

11.4 Consent to Financing Not Unreasonably Withheld. No Member shall unreasonably withhold, condition, or delay consent to a bona fide third-party financing (whether at the LLC or Company level) that is reasonably necessary for the Venture’s operations or growth and that does not disproportionately or adversely affect such Member relative to the other Members. This Clause does not require any Member to consent to a financing on terms that are commercially unreasonable in light of prevailing market conditions, that disproportionately dilutes or burdens one Member, that materially impairs a Member’s rights under this Agreement, or that contains unreasonable terms; and in all cases dilution shall be borne pro rata under Clause 6.2, and no Member’s rights under this Agreement may be stripped, reduced, or subordinated without that Member’s written consent.

12. Transfers of Membership Interests

12.1 Lock-In. No Member may transfer, pledge, or encumber any Membership Interest except as permitted by this Clause 12. The same restrictions apply, mutatis mutandis, to any transfer by the LLC of Group Entity Securities (see Clause 9.6).

12.2 Right of First Refusal. A Member wishing to sell must first offer the interest to the other Members pro rata, on the same terms as the proposed third-party sale, by written notice. The other Members have [REQUIRED: 30 OR OTHER] days to accept.

12.3 Tag-Along (Minority Protection). Completion guide — Cofounder Agreement v2

Version 2.0 — Public Review Draft | Prepared September 19, 2026

This guide is educational and non-operative. It is not part of the agreement. The package is an unapproved review draft, not a signing-ready legal opinion. Version 1 remains available; this public review draft does not amend existing agreements. Read DISCLAIMER.md and obtain California/Delaware company counsel and tax advice.

1. Intended structure

Two original cofounders share a California LLC, taxed as a partnership, which holds their operating-company shares. The Delaware corporation operates and raises money. The LLC is a holding/governance vehicle, not the operating business. Later founders normally receive separately issued Company securities directly or through their own vehicles. Admission to the shared LLC is a different decision requiring unanimous consent.

The purpose is to align the minority and majority original cofounders within the shared block. If their internal split is 30/70 and external financing leaves their LLC with 60% of the corporation, their indirect stakes are 18/42 while their relative split stays 30/70. Company dilution and LLC membership dilution are different transactions.

Separate holdings for later participants do not authorize either original founder to bypass the shared block. Clause 6.6 addresses personal equity, nominees, affiliated entities, revenue participation, and successor businesses. Legitimately approved compensation remains possible. The LLC does not guarantee equal after-tax outcomes.

2. Complete the fields and companion documents

Complete every REQUIRED field, select every SELECT ONE alternative, and remove unused options and instructions from a signing copy. Preserve a copy of the completed schedules and all authorized updates. A blank is not an affirmative election.

Schedule A: repeat the member record for each actual LLC member, reconcile percentages to 100%, document consideration and recipient, identify background IP, and select no vesting or complete vesting terms. Later participants holding only Company stock do not appear as LLC members. Additional members need approval and a deed of adherence.

Schedule B: select the supermajority and board-right thresholds and financial limits; reconcile them with the defined protected minority consent. The ordinary vote does not override a reserved matter. A qualifying drag remains governed by its specific threshold and safeguards. The expert cannot create missing consent under the revised deadlock clause.

Schedule C: assign company and holding-vehicle duties separately; appoint the tax coordinator, preparer, advisers, and backups. Set the issuance-review dates and personal-election register. Responsibility for filing a personal election stays with the taxpayer.

Schedule D: maintain an entity file, one record per corporate stock lot, actual acquisition-date tax ownership, annual reviews, advances, and transaction comparisons. Administrative corrections do not amend rights. Maintain a confidential preparer file for private tax information and circulate only needed conclusions.

Before issuance, coordinate California LLC formation and this operating agreement; Delaware charter/bylaws; board and stockholder consents; stockholders’/voting agreements; membership and stock purchase documents; securities exemptions and notices; IP assignments; employment and contractor documents; vesting and repurchase terms; capitalization ledgers; and tax registrations/elections. This agreement is not a replacement for that package. In particular, the corporation and incoming holders must execute appropriate instruments for protected corporate rights; an agreement between LLC members alone does not bind strangers or supersede directors’ duties.

3. QSBS: statutory rules versus contractual precautions

Section 1202(g) permits qualifying pass-through stock gain for eligible owners. The member generally must own the LLC interest when the LLC acquires the stock and continuously afterward; eligible gain is limited by the interest held at acquisition. The holding LLC does not automatically create corporate double taxation or a single shared exclusion cap. Separate qualifying taxpayers apply their own limits.

For qualifying stock acquired after July 4, 2025, the federal exclusion tiers are 50% after at least three years, 75% after four, and 100% after five. The fixed eligible-gain base is $15 million per taxpayer/issuer, coordinated with prior gains, with a ten-times-qualifying-basis alternative. The percentage applies to capped eligible gain; the partial exclusion can consume the full eligible-gain amount against fixed capacity. The gross-asset ceiling for stock issued after July 4, 2025 is $75 million under special tax measures. New dollar amounts are indexed after 2026. Older shares have different vintage rules. Do not treat these figures as a guarantee or write them into a permanent contractual warranty. 26 USC 1202.

The issuer must meet original-issuance, C-corporation, gross-assets, active-business, and other conditions. Industry exclusions, working-capital limits, and repurchases can matter. Later founders can use new original issuances, but issuer eligibility must still be tested then. A single-member LLC without a corporate election is generally disregarded federally; a multi-member LLC brings its own pass-through conditions. IRS single-member LLC guidance; redemption regulation.

The agreement’s annual reviews, approval gates, stock-lot schedule, seven-year post-disposition retention floor, and deadlines for professional information are contractual precautions. They are not IRS certification requirements or safe harbors. Reasonable efforts do not guarantee exclusion or prohibit an approved superior commercial exit.

California does not conform to the QSBS exclusion or Section 1045 rollover. Compute state consequences separately. An excluded federal gain can still require significant California tax cash. FTB Schedule D instructions.

4. Vesting and issuance responsibilities

No vesting is applied by default. A possible negotiated schedule is four years with a one-year cliff and monthly vesting thereafter, but acceleration and repurchase terms must be chosen explicitly. Schedule A and separate issuance documents must agree.

For membership vesting, establish the founder’s tax partner status and economic interest when the LLC acquires each stock lot. An LLC-interest Section 83(b) election does not elect on underlying corporate stock or by itself establish QSBS eligibility. Stock issued for services, contributions of IP, and differing dates at the two levels need advice before transfer. A Section 83(b) election generally has a 30-day deadline measured from property transfer; obtain filing evidence and furnish required copies. IRS Form 15620.

Repurchase or forfeiture can change percentages and eligible gain in existing stock and may implicate stock-redemption rules depending on the transaction. Clause 6.4 now expressly accommodates the agreed vesting mechanism while preserving its limits. Do not backdate membership, stock issuance, or elections.

5. Common tax advances and accounting

Clause 7.4 uses a common assumed federal/California rate set by income category, rather than paying each member’s actual tax. It uses highest applicable marginal individual rates and only reduces the federal base for QSBS to the extent the preparer supports the exclusion for every member for that gain. This conservative liquidity convention preserves pro rata cash sharing; it can overfund a member with lower actual tax and does not assure full coverage for a member with different tax allocations or other circumstances.

Example: a 30/70 LLC has cumulative assumed tax of $100,000 and has already distributed $40,000 of cash that year. Its additional advance target is $60,000, paid $18,000/$42,000 if lawful cash is available. Cumulative distributions are $30,000/$70,000. If total cumulative distributable economics later equal $200,000, the prior $100,000 is counted once and the remaining $100,000 is $30,000/$70,000. Advances do not create an additional $100,000 entitlement.

If the estimate later falls, reduce future payments; no ordinary personal repayment obligation arises merely from overestimation. Document settlement before ownership changes and final liquidation. Statutory withholding is attributed to the relevant member and reconciled with economic entitlements; it is not authority for an elective entity-level tax. Cash constraints or statutory restrictions can delay advances. Reserves and methodology changes require unanimous consent, while mandatory law applies regardless of agreement. California Corporations Code 17704.04–17704.06.

The common advance model is intentionally not a full bespoke partnership tax allocation appendix. Counsel/preparer must supply any additional capital-account, Section 704(c), varying-interest, regulatory allocation, liquidation, and audit provisions required for actual contributions and economic arrangements before the affected transaction. These cannot be used to amend minority economics silently. 26 USC 704; 26 USC 706.

6. Exits, transfers, and deadlock

An LLC sale of qualifying corporate shares can pass eligible gain through. A member’s sale of their LLC interest generally cannot claim QSBS on that interest. Corporate asset sales can leave corporate-level tax even if shareholder liquidation gain qualifies. Clause 12.7 requires a written comparison before binding approval and a ten-business-day review period, reducible only by every affected member. It is not a tax veto beyond existing consent rights or a promise of equal after-tax proceeds.

Section 1045 may defer qualifying early-sale gain through replacement QSBS when its holding, reinvestment, election, and 60-day requirements are met; partnership rules need particular attention. 26 USC 1045; partnership regulation.

Underlying-stock distributions need unanimous consent, tax analysis, pro rata economics unless affected members agree otherwise, and corporate protections effective at transfer. Section 1202(h) may preserve qualifying distributions to eligible partners; it is not a general guarantee. Gifts of LLC interests and contributions of personal stock to a partnership require separate analysis.

The original fair-value buy-sell mechanism remains for its specified existential deadlocks. It can still compel a sale under its express terms and poses liquidity and funding tradeoffs; counsel should assess those before signing. The expert’s authority is now limited to factual questions and cannot override protected consent or amend rights. Operational departure should also be coordinated with California dissociation law; leaving a job and ceasing to be a legal member are not interchangeable.

7. Specific legal-review priorities

Confirm California nonwaivable LLC rules, fiduciary duties, information rights, lawful distributions, member dissociation, and remedies. Confirm Delaware charter and stockholder-agreement implementation, director duties, investor acceptance, and enforceability of protective rights. California Corporations Code 17701.10; Delaware corporate governance provisions.

The prior general post-exit restraint language has been replaced with loyalty and trade-secret protection. A passive holding LLC’s existence does not establish a statutory noncompete exception. Any proposed sale/exit restraint requires separate fact-specific drafting and current employment-law review. Also review IP assignment exclusions and third-party rights. California Business and Professions Code 16600; Section 16602.5.

Ohio remains a future alternative, not an option within this California draft. A different formation state requires a separate internal-governance rewrite and analysis of actual management and California nexus. It does not change California-resident members’ personal taxation by itself. FTB doing-business guidance.

9. September 19 revision — services, awards, and closing

Complete service terms in Schedule C before work begins: compensation and its start, time and availability, authority, resources, expenses, outside activities, and optional severance. A title or equity award does not establish a wage-law exemption. No-cash and deferred arrangements need individualized review; do not infer unpaid service from a blank. California wage guidance.

Milestones are optional and separate from base founder equity. Complete every selected award record before approval or issuance. Identify the denominator: a percentage of a founder LLC is not the same as that percentage of the operating company. State both achievement and any subsequent service vesting, company-controlled dependencies, delay relief, partial-credit choice, and each exit event. The independent expert resolves factual acceptance, not missing commercial terms or consent. Incomplete proposals neither create awards nor forfeit existing interests. Do not impose a sales/customer outcome on a technical founder without expressly allocating its dependencies and risk.

Complete payment provisions in the operative clauses and reconcile every schedule. Do not replace a separately negotiated cash closing with installments inadvertently.

Document the actual owner, permitted use, fees, sublicensing, transfer/change-of-control rights and post-departure continuity. Investor-ready ownership does not mean claiming ownership of open-source, customer data or affiliate technology. An existing assignment is not automatically unwound by this revision. California Labor Code 2870.

Use Clause 5.4 and Schedule D to coordinate equity and asset steps. If work starts early, sign a lawful interim arrangement with the responsible parties, dates, compensation, IP terms and remedies. NewCo must obtain rights from actual owners; formation alone moves neither assets nor contracts. An agreed service-credit date is not a property-transfer date. Have advisers identify each taxpayer and actual transfer for any election; the statutory 83(b) deadline is generally 30 days after transfer. 26 USC 83.
